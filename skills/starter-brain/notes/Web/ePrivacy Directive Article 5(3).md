# ePrivacy Directive Article 5(3)

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Rule: storing or accessing information on the device (cookies, local storage, pixels, fingerprinting) only with consent after clear and comprehensive information.
> Exemptions: purely for transmitting a communication, or strictly necessary for a service the user explicitly asked for.
> Each member state transposes it; example (Belgium): art. 10/2 of the law of 30 July 2018, enforced by the APD.

## The text (EUR-Lex, consolidated Directive 2002/58/EC, version of 19.12.2009, Article 5(3))
Member States must ensure that storing information, or gaining access to information already stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user is only allowed if the user **has given consent, having been provided with clear and comprehensive information** (in line with data-protection law), notably about the purposes.

**Exemptions:** technical storage or access
- for the sole purpose of carrying out the transmission of a communication over an electronic communications network, or
- **strictly necessary** for an information society service **explicitly requested** by the user to provide that service.

## What it covers
Not just cookies: any reading or writing on the device (local storage, pixels, device fingerprinting). Consent quality follows the GDPR definition (freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous).

## National transposition — example (Belgium)
The Belgian APD's APD cookie checklist cites article 10/2 of the law of 30 July 2018 (the 'loi-cadre') as the Belgian rule and reads it strictly: audience measurement is in principle not 'strictly necessary'.

## How to use it
Apply it to every script and embed: analytics, heatmaps, video embeds (YouTube embeds and cookie consent), chat widgets, fonts loaded from third parties. Example checklist and overview (Belgium): Playbook - Cookie compliance checklist Belgium; Belgian cookie rules.
