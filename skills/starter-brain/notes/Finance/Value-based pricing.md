# Value-based pricing

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Price follows the value to the client, not your effort. Cost-plus tells you where the price must not go below.

## Definition
Prices set primarily, but not exclusively, on the value perceived or estimated by the customer (marketing textbook wording via search snippet). Baker: value is set before the work begins; clients buy outcomes, not hours (IFAC - Baker on value pricing for professional firms).

## Evidence and limits
- Advocacy from an accountants' federation publication; no data comparing profit under hourly vs value pricing was found (open gap).
- Practitioner guides say it fits a well-defined deliverable; with unknown scope use a capped or hybrid fee (not independently verified).
- Value estimates are assumptions: write them down and revisit after each project.

## Steps
1. Ask what the result is worth to the client (revenue gained, cost avoided, risk removed). 2. Compute your Cost-plus pricing floor. 3. Offer 2-3 options at different scope and price (Fixed price, time and materials and retainers). 4. After delivery, log actual hours and margin to learn (Checklist - Margin check before quoting).
