# Cash vs profit vs revenue

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Revenue = sales recognised; profit = revenue minus costs (accrual); cash = bank movements.
> They differ by timing: an invoice is revenue today but cash only when paid.
> Run the business on a cash forecast, judge it on profit; both are needed.

## Definitions
- **Revenue (turnover)**: value of sales invoiced in a period, whether or not paid.
- **Profit**: revenue minus the costs that belong to the period (accrual accounting). Costs such as depreciation reduce profit without any cash leaving.
- **Cash flow**: money actually received minus money actually paid in a period. Timing follows *payment dates*, not invoice dates: a purchase made in January and payable in March is a cash outflow in March (Bpifrance Création, Feb 2025).

## Where they differ
| Event | Revenue / profit | Cash |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice sent on 30-day terms | Today | In ~30+ days |
| Supplier bill received, paid later | Cost today | When paid |
| Equipment bought | Spread as depreciation | Whole price at purchase |
| Loan received | Not revenue | Cash in (debt, not income) |
| Loan repayment (capital) | Not a cost | Cash out |
| Tax and VAT payments | Timing differs from the period they relate to | On due dates |

## Why it matters
A business can show a profit and still be unable to pay a bill; see Why profitable companies run out of cash. The bridge between the two is working capital: Working capital and the cash conversion cycle.

## Steps
1. Keep two views: the P&L (profit) and a cash forecast ([[Cash forecasting - 13-week and 12-month]]).
2. Each month, list the gap between profit and the change in bank balance and explain it (receivables up? loan repayment? tax paid?).
3. Decide with the cash view; judge performance with the profit view.

Not financial advice: ask an accountant how your own accounts treat these items.
