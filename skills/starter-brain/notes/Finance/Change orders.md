# Change orders

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Every out-of-scope request gets written, priced for cost AND schedule, and approved before work.
> Approval by the person with budget authority; keep the original quote unchanged and add the order.
> Template: Template - Change order form.

## Principle
Integrated change control (PMBOK): a change to scope can push out schedule and raise cost; approved baselines change only through the process. Thresholds in blog examples are illustrative, not PMI standards.

## Steps
1. Capture the request in writing (who, what, why). 2. Check against the scope statement (Checklist - Scope statement and exclusions). 3. Estimate hours with O/M/P (Three-point estimation (PERT)). 4. Price at the change-order rate (usually the sell rate; rush = Rush fees). 5. State schedule impact. 6. Send the form; wait for signature or written approval. 7. Update the project budget and Template - Estimate vs actual log.

## Rules of thumb (judgment)
- Tiny changes (< 1 h) can be absorbed once, said out loud, to build goodwill; track them.
- Say in the original quote that additions are priced separately (Template - Project quote with margin check).
See also Scope creep - what it costs and how to price it.
