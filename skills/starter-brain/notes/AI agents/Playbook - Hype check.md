# Playbook - Hype check

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Announced (first-party or press) is not the same as claimed (posts).
> Revenue claims stay candidates until verified.
> AI-written trending summaries are leads, not sources.

## Checklist
- Who says it: the company, the press, or a third party?
- Is the number verified (Stripe-linked leaderboard) or a screenshot?
- Is churn mentioned? See Signal - Churn after the launch spike.
- 'My friend made…' or course funnel? See Signal - Income claims in AI agency content.
- Fake scarcity ('3 seats left')?
- Is the source an AI-generated trending summary?

## Added after verification (2026-10-22)
- **Verified is not profitable:** a Stripe-linked board proves payments only (Signal - Verified revenue as a trust layer). Ask for refunds, churn and cost.
- **Benchmark before believing:** AI consumer apps retain worse than non-AI apps (RevenueCat 2026), so a headline MRR with no retention is incomplete.
- **Income claims can be unlawful:** the FTC's Operation AI Comply (from Sept 2024) targeted 'AI makes you money' business-opportunity schemes; Click Profit (banned, Aug 2025) and Air AI (complaint Aug 2025) are the cases. Source: law-firm summaries — check ftc.gov for case status.
- **Fake scarcity:** UCPD Annex I (EU) bars falsely claiming limited availability; the UK CMA treats resetting countdowns and 'only 5 left' without a real shortage as misleading. Law-firm summaries; confirm on EUR-Lex before relying on it.
- **Sold for X?** Find the listing or the acquirer's statement; a post saying 'sold' is a lead (the $85k GEO-tracker sale found no corroboration).
- **Predictions are not data:** e.g. Gartner's -25% search volume forecast.
