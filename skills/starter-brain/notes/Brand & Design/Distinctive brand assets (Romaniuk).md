# Distinctive brand assets (Romaniuk)

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Asset = logo, colour, shape, sound, character, tagline or pack that makes people think of the brand.
> Measure fame (share of category buyers who link it to the brand) and uniqueness (links to no other brand).
> Test unprompted: prompting can inflate fame (a figure of about 20 points is unverified). Changing assets usually weakens them.

## What it is
Building Distinctive Brand Assets (Jenni Romaniuk, Oxford University Press, 16 March 2018 per two bookseller listings; library records say 2018) gives a method: pick from a palette of asset types, measure, then protect.

## Measures
- **Fame:** share of category buyers who think of the brand when they meet the asset.
- **Uniqueness:** how few other brands the asset also brings to mind.
- Prompting respondents can inflate fame, so test unprompted. A figure of up to about 20 percentage points came from a search-result summary and was not found again on 2026-11-03: unverified.

## Change
Romaniuk's position: changing assets typically sets a brand back; assets should not be fashion items. This is the strongest argument against frequent logo refreshes; see Rebrand vs refresh.

## Caveat
I could not open the book. Details come from publisher listings and event reports. The Ehrenberg-Bass research uses mostly consumer-goods categories.

## Check list for a studio
- [ ] List every asset the client uses (colour, shape, logo, sound, character, line, pack).
- [ ] Run an unprompted fame/uniqueness test on 100+ category buyers if budget allows.
- [ ] Keep the top 2-3 assets stable across touchpoints.
