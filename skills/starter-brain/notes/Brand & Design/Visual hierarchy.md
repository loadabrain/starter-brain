# Visual hierarchy

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Hierarchy = the designed order of attention: one first thing, a clear second, then detail.
> Tools: contrast, scale, colour, proximity/grouping, position; use fewer, stronger differences.
> Supported by perception research (pop-out, grouping); the exact recipes are practitioner convention. Check with a squint test and 5-second test.

## Definition
NN/g describes visual hierarchy as using contrast and colour (and scale, grouping) so the page communicates what matters first; once a hierarchy exists, variations of colour/contrast, scale or grouping express levels (Kelley Gordon, NN/g, 17 Jan 2021, opened 2026-11-03: factors are colour and contrast, scale, and grouping by proximity, white space and enclosures; it recommends the squint test).

## Levers
- **Scale**: larger reads first; enlarging too many things dilutes it.
- **Contrast/colour**: strongest differences win; never colour alone (colour-blind viewers) and check the 4.5:1 text contrast of WCAG for text.
- **Proximity/grouping**: see [[Gestalt grouping principles]].
- **Position/alignment**: top-left in left-to-right scripts is habit, not law; see F-pattern and how people scan pages.
- **Isolation**: see Isolation effect (von Restorff) and Preattentive features and visual search.

## Test
Squint or blur the layout: the first three things you see should be the three that matter. Ask 5 people what they saw first. NN/g recommends user checks.

## Trust & evidence
Mechanisms: high (vision science). Recipes: medium (practitioner). Checklist: Checklist - Visual hierarchy. Also Lupton & Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics* (Princeton Architectural Press) treats hierarchy, scale, rhythm and balance as basic organising tools.
