# Gestalt grouping principles

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Viewers group elements by proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, symmetry, common region and connectedness before they read anything.
> The principles are well studied in vision science (Wagemans et al. 2012), but each is a tendency that can be outweighed by others, not a law.
> Use them to make groups obvious with the least ink: space and alignment first, boxes and rules last.

## What the evidence supports
The 2012 two-part review in *Psychological Bulletin* (Wagemans, Elder, Kubovy, Palmer, Peterson, Singh, von der Heydt) covers the classical grouping principles: proximity, similarity, common fate, good continuation, closure, symmetry and parallelism. It also covers newer ones: synchrony, common region, and element/uniform connectedness. It links grouping to figure-ground organisation and to neural work on contour integration. Part I is *Psychological Bulletin* 138(6):1172-1217 (PMID 22845751). A second article covers the theoretical foundations (simplicity vs likelihood); its author list was not re-checked.

## Design use (my synthesis, not a quote)
| Principle | Plain meaning | Design move |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity | Close things belong together | Group by spacing before adding lines or boxes |
| Similarity | Alike things belong together | Same style = same role |
| Common region | Things inside one boundary belong together | Cards, panels, background fields |
| Connectedness | Things joined by a line belong together | Timelines, diagrams |
| Continuity | The eye follows smooth paths | Align edges so lines run through |
| Closure | We complete incomplete shapes | Logos with gaps (e.g. negative-space marks) |
| Common fate | Things moving together group | Motion design |
| Symmetry | Symmetric parts read as one object | Balanced marks; asymmetry draws attention |

## Limits
- Principles compete (e.g. proximity vs similarity); the stronger cue wins and strength is context dependent. Test on a squint or a 5-second glance.
- Evidence is mostly from simple stimuli in labs, not full layouts; treat applied claims as informed practice.
