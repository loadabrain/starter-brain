# Checklist - Font licence before handover

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Inventory every font in every deliverable, including those hidden in templates and PDFs.
> Match each use (print, web, app, video, social, packaging) to a licence the client holds.
> Transfer or re-buy licences in the client's name; record proof.

## Checklist
- [ ] 1. List every typeface and weight used in logos, templates, documents, site, app, social, video titles.
- [ ] 2. Mark source: foundry purchase, subscription (Adobe Fonts etc.), open licence (OFL), system font, custom.
- [ ] 3. List client uses planned for 12 months: print, web, app, ePub, video/broadcast, merchandise, ads.
- [ ] 4. For each font and use, find the EULA clause and note limits (users, pageviews, titles). See Font licence types - desktop, web, app, broadcast, logo.
- [ ] 5. Subscription fonts (Adobe Fonts): the client needs their own access if they will edit files; otherwise deliver outlines/PDFs. See Adobe Fonts licence terms.
- [ ] 6. Fonts you bought: check whether the licence can be transferred; many foundries want the licence issued to the end client. Buy in the client's name or get written transfer.
- [ ] 7. Logo: confirm logo use is permitted and outlines are allowed. See Fonts in logos and trademarks.
- [ ] 8. Web: pageview tier fits the traffic; self-host WOFF2 unless a hosted service is licensed. See Google Fonts GDPR ruling (LG Munich I, 2022).
- [ ] 9. Language coverage for the client's markets: Checklist - Language support.
- [ ] 10. Hand over: licence documents, invoices, font files allowed by the EULA, a filled Template - Font licence register, renewal dates for subscriptions.

## If a licence is missing
Buy the upgrade before launch; or swap to an open font (OFL) with Decision guide - Choosing a typeface for a brand.

## Evidence
Steps follow the mistakes named by TypeTogether and Alphabettes (trial fonts in finals, desktop used for web/app, shifting terms). Practice, not law. Step 5 re-checked 2026-11-03 against Adobe's FAQ excerpts: a client who must edit with the fonts needs their own licence; rasterised or properly embedded output needs none. Step 2 'system font': Microsoft's position on fonts bundled with Windows or Office depends on the product licence and is not settled by sources read here (open gap); for a client job prefer a font with a clear licence. Also check unlicensed 'free' downloads: Checklist - Free fonts and the personal-use trap.
