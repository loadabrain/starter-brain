# Landing page anatomy for a service business

> [!summary] TL;DR
> One page, one offer, one primary action; Google rates landing page experience on usefulness, relevance, navigation and match to the ad.
> People scan: lead with the outcome and put proof and key facts where the eye lands first.
> Show price or a range, contact details and the guarantee early; NN/g says upfront disclosure builds trust.

## Explanation
A service landing page is the single page an ad click lands on. Google's Search Ads 360 help describes landing page experience as one factor in Quality Score, made up of how useful and relevant the information is, how easy the page is to navigate, the number of links, and whether the page meets the expectations set by the clicked ad. NN/g's research on reading (Nielsen 1997; Pernice 2017) says most visitors scan, so the first screen and the first words of each heading carry the page. NN/g's trust research (Harley, 2016) adds four credibility factors: design quality, upfront disclosure of contacts, prices and guarantees, comprehensive and current content, and a connection to the wider web such as third-party reviews.

A working order (vault synthesis): 1. headline with outcome and the named buyer; 2. one proof (number, clip or review); 3. how it works in three steps; 4. price or range; 5. reviews and examples; 6. guarantee or risk removal; 7. FAQ with the real objections; 8. one form or one call button, repeated at the end. Remove the main navigation menu so the only exits are the button and the legal links.

## How to use it
Use Playbook - Landing page checklist. Match the headline to the ad's promise in the same words, put the phone number as a tap-to-call button on mobile, and keep the form as short as possible (Forms and friction on lead pages).
**Cost:** free if you build it yourself; hosting costs a few euros a month. **Metric:** landing page conversion rate (form or call per click), bounce, scroll depth. **Cheap test:** show the page to five people from the target profile and watch them find the price and the button (Why five users reveal most usability problems); then A/B test headlines with a fixed sample (AB testing basics and why not to peek).

## Trust & evidence
Sources are Google help (high for how Google describes the factors; the SA360 page gives no rating scale or improvement steps) and NN/g (high trust, qualitative studies with small samples). The eight-step order is the vault's synthesis and has no controlled-test source. Confidence: medium.
