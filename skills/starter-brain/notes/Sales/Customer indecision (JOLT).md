# Customer indecision (JOLT)

> [!summary] TL;DR
> 40–60% of deals end in 'no decision' (the authors' own figures, 2.5M calls; not independently checked). Cause is often fear of messing up, not love of the status quo.
> Do not raise fear: the authors report it backfired in 84% of cases (low confidence). Reduce risk and recommend instead.

## Key facts
- The study covers more than 2.5 million recorded sales conversations, transactional and complex, taken from web-conferencing platforms and analysed with machine learning (8,300 factors) [1][5][3]. The earlier wording "from several dozen companies" is not on any page read and was dropped.
- The authors' own figures: 40–60% of deals end with buyers who intended to buy but failed to act. 87% of customers showed moderate or high indecision (blog, re-read 2026-11-11); "almost 90%" in the interview [2][3].
- Two causes: status quo preference, and indecision from fear of failure; the research frames the second as FOMU (fear of messing up) rather than FOMO [5]. A breakdown into three named fears is not confirmed on the pages read and is left out.
- JOLT: **J**udge the level of indecision; **O**ffer a recommendation; **L**imit the exploration (more demos, references and whitepapers feed paralysis); **T**ake risk off the table (opt-outs, pro-rated refunds, change clauses) [2][3].
- The authors say high performers who address indecision convert up to 57% of deals (their own figure) [2].
- Backfire claim: when buyers waver and reps re-argue the case against the status quo, hype fear or lean on discounts, the chance of losing rose in 84% of those interactions. The three sources word it differently ("backfired 84% of the time"; "works 16%, fails 84%"), and "backfire" is not defined [4][5][6]. Challenger Inc: "In 84% of these interactions, this pattern increased the likelihood the deal would be lost"; The Quota: "an 84% probability" of raising the odds the customer does nothing [5][3].

## Details
- In discovery, ask how they made similar decisions and what worries them about this one; use pain questions to understand, not to frighten (Sandler pain funnel, the brain's own judgment).

## When it doesn't apply
- Author-run research promoting a book and training; no dataset, definitions or models published in the pages read. Figures are the authors' (low confidence for the 84% claim).
- Buyers with a real status-quo preference need a case for change, not risk reduction.
