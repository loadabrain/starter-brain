# Mutual action plan

> [!summary] TL;DR
> A co-created document (also called close plan or mutual success plan) listing what, who and when, working back from the buyer's go-live.
> Include procurement, legal and security steps with micro-deadlines. The buyer's willingness to co-own it is a qualification signal.

## Key facts
- A mutual action plan (MAP) is shared between seller and buyer to set expectations through the sales cycle [1].
- A close plan covers the path from verbal interest to signed contract; the best versions are built with the buyer, not handed to them [2].
- Milestones work back from a date the buyer already cares about (budget cycle, launch, compliance deadline) [2].
- Slow stages get their own micro-milestones: first redlines, response, final (legal), security review, procurement, RFP/RFQ [2].
- Pushback on a draft is a sign of buy-in [2].

## Template (Salesforce + HubSpot)
1. **Value statement:** "This plan supports [Buyer] in evaluating [Seller] to achieve [goal] by [date]." [1]
2. **Stakeholders** on both sides: names, roles, who signs, who approves budget; include unknown roles to ask about [1][2].
3. **Milestones** back from the go-live date [2].
4. **Action items** with an owner on each side and a deadline [1][2].
5. **Micro-milestones** for legal, security, procurement [2].
6. **Post-signature section:** onboarding and expected outcomes [1].

## How to run it
- Introduce it once the buyer confirms interest; walk through a rough draft and invite corrections [2].
- Ask: "When you last bought something like this, what were the steps? How long did legal take?" [2]
- Review it at every meeting and set a recurring check-in [2].
- Pair with [[Calibrated questions]]: "What happens on your side between today and signature?" (the brain's own application)
- A MAP names buyer-side people: store it with access controls and only the data needed, under GDPR / UK GDPR or US state privacy rules (the brain's own judgment).

## When it doesn't apply
- Both sources are CRM vendors; their tool claims (e.g. HubSpot's "48% decrease in time to close") are unverified vendor statistics and are not used here.
- Small, transactional deals do not need one.
- Public tenders fix the timeline for you; see Public procurement thresholds and standstill (EU, UK, US).
