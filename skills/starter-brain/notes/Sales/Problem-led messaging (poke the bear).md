# Problem-led messaging (poke the bear)

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Leading questions make prospects defensive; a neutral question about a hidden risk gets them thinking.
> Works as a cold email CTA, a call pivot and a discovery opener.

## Key facts
- Braun says leading questions ("If I could show you how to make more money, would you be interested?") fall flat because prospects "smell commission breath" [1].
- His alternative is a neutral question that makes them think differently about their current solution: "What do you know that your prospect might not know that can hurt them?" [1]
- His examples follow a "How do you know ...?" pattern: "If you run MVRs quarterly, how do you know violations aren't occurring between pulls?"; "How do you know you're not being overcharged for shipping?" [1]
- In his CTA article he replaces time asks ("Are you free Tuesday at 2?") with such questions, e.g. "How are you attracting top SDRs that don't respond to job postings?" [2]
- No controlled data exists for the technique; support is practitioner experience (the brain's own judgment: test it on your own list).

## Details
How to build one (the brain's own synthesis):
1. List what your best customers did before they bought: the status quo.
2. Name the gap or risk they could not see.
3. Write it as a neutral "How do you know…" question with no adjectives or claims.
4. Tone test: if "we're fine" would feel comfortable to answer, it is neutral enough.

## When it doesn't apply
- If you cannot name a real, provable risk, the question reads as a trick.
- With a buyer already running a search, answer their stated need instead.
