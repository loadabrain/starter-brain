# Pain → impact → urgency

> [!summary] TL;DR
> A problem alone is not a reason to buy: buyers live with problems. Develop impact, quantify it, then find the date that makes waiting costly.
> Don't manufacture fear: JOLT data say pushing fear of missing out often increases 'no decision'.

## Key facts
- Step 1, **problem**: a specific business problem with a root cause (Problem Identification Chart). Huthwaite: people live with problems, so a problem mentioned is not a reason to buy [1].
- Step 2, **impact**: what the problem does to the organisation and people. SPIN's Implication questions do this [1]; Keenan says "impact is where urgency, value and need live" (quoted by Gong) [2]; Force Management calls it negative consequences [3].
- Step 3, **quantification**: put numbers on it together ([[Quantifying business value]]); Sandler says the pain step is not complete without a monetary cost agreed with the buyer [4].
- Step 4, **urgency**: a critical date, event or cost of delay; the Great Demo! Situation slide asks for a Critical Date [5].
- With indecisive buyers, raising fear of missing out tends to backfire; reducing fear of messing up works better [6] ([[Customer indecision (JOLT)]]).
- Gong: winning discovery calls dig into 3–4 business problems [7].

## Details
Question patterns (the brain's own wording): problem, "Where does the current process break down?"; impact, "Who else feels it? What does it cost in a typical month?"; urgency, "What happens if this is the same in six months? Is there a date that matters?"; priority, "Where does this rank against your other projects?" (CHAMP).

## When it doesn't apply
- Huthwaite found implied needs alone did not predict success in large sales; the chain must end in a need the buyer states (SPIN Selling) [1].
- Buyers with no budget cycle or compelling event may stay stuck at step 3; that is a qualification signal, not a pressure opportunity.
