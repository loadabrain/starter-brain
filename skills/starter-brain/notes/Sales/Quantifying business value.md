# Quantifying business value

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Do the maths with the buyer, using their numbers; don't present a vendor ROI they never saw.
> Forrester's TEI structure: costs, benefits, flexibility, risk.
> Great Demo!'s 'Delta': cheaper, better, faster, or couldn't be done before; measured in time, people, money.

## Key facts
- Sandler: for each problem ask what it cost in hard money over the past year and the coming months, then compare TCO, ROI or risk side by side with the buyer [1].
- Forrester's Total Economic Impact models cost, benefits, flexibility and risk; commissioned TEIs are labelled as such and based on customer interviews, so a vendor-paid study is not independent proof [2].
- Force Management: tangible Positive Business Outcomes measured by KPIs the customer will use after go-live (Command of the Message) [3].
- Great Demo! asks for the Delta, measured in time, people and money and sorted as cheaper, better, faster or "couldn't be done before" (Great Demo! method) [4].
- Anderson, Narus & van Rossum argue suppliers must show superior value in terms customers believe (preview only) [5].

## Credibility rules (the brain's own judgment)
1. Use the buyer's figures; mark your assumptions.
2. Show a range (low / expected / high), not one heroic number.
3. Include the buyer's internal costs (time, change, training).
4. Separate hard savings from soft ones.
5. Let the buyer present it internally; buyers fear not getting promised returns ([[Customer indecision (JOLT)]]).

## When it doesn't apply
- Buyers who cannot or will not share baseline data: quantify with ranges and say so.
- Commodity or compliance purchases, where the value is avoiding a penalty rather than a gain.
- Sources are practitioner and vendor methods, not controlled studies.
